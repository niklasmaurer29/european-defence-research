# European Defence peer memo — FY2025 historical screen

**Universe:** Rheinmetall, Hensoldt and RENK  
**Financial period:** FY2025  
**Market-data date:** 31 December 2025  
**Purpose:** educational equity- and credit-research exercise; not investment
advice, and not a buy, hold or sell recommendation.

## Executive view

The three companies offered substantial disclosed order coverage at FY2025
year-end, but the quality of that coverage and the balance-sheet implications
differ materially. Rheinmetall combined the largest disclosed backlog and net
liquidity, but its 42.4x EV / EBIT screen required sustained profitable
conversion and careful treatment of framework agreements. Hensoldt's
defence-electronics order book supported an earnings-growth case, while its
lease-inclusive debt and lower interest-coverage diagnostic made execution and
debt service the key diligence point. RENK screened at the lowest EV / EBIT
multiple, but weaker free-cash-flow conversion meant that the apparent discount
could not be interpreted without working-capital analysis.

## Peer snapshot

| Metric | Rheinmetall | Hensoldt | RENK |

| EV / EBIT | 42.4x | 41.7x | 33.7x |

\*Rheinmetall's stated backlog includes framework agreements; its 6.4x ratio
is therefore not mechanically comparable with the other issuers' figures.

## Valuation and credit interpretation

The valuation screen uses a common 31 December 2025 market-data date and
calculates enterprise value as market capitalisation plus standardised net debt.
Standardised net debt includes lease liabilities where the underlying data was
available. To avoid false precision, the screen uses IFRS EBIT rather than
EV / EBITDA: the disclosed profitability measures are not yet fully
harmonised across the peer set.

| Company | What the historical screen indicates | Diligence question before a conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Rheinmetall | The highest valuation multiple coincides with the strongest disclosed liquidity and the largest order coverage. | Does the disclosed backlog translate into high-margin deliveries once framework agreements are separated from binding orders? |
| Hensoldt | The valuation is close to Rheinmetall's, despite lower interest coverage and lease-inclusive net debt. | Can earnings growth and order-book conversion preserve comfortable debt service through the funding cycle? |
| RENK | The lowest multiple comes with solid order coverage and moderate interest coverage, but lower FY2025 free cash flow. | Does working capital normalise sufficiently for free-cash-flow conversion to recover? |

## Catalysts and risks to monitor

### Rheinmetall

- **Catalyst:** profitable conversion of the disclosed order book into revenue,
  operating profit and cash flow.
- **Risk:** disappointing delivery or margins would be amplified by the premium
  valuation; framework agreements need separate treatment from binding orders.

### Hensoldt

- **Catalyst:** order-book conversion into earnings while the refinanced funding
  structure remains within covenant terms.
- **Risk:** operational underperformance has less headroom given the 2.1x
  EBIT / gross-interest diagnostic and lease-inclusive net debt.

### RENK

- **Catalyst:** normalisation of inventories, trade receivables and contract
  assets, supporting a rebound in free-cash-flow conversion.
- **Risk:** continued working-capital absorption could justify a valuation
  discount, even if the order backlog remains high.

## Conclusion and next diligence step

The historical screen does not establish a "winner." It establishes a focused
research agenda: backlog quality for Rheinmetall, debt service for Hensoldt,
and cash conversion for RENK. The next analytical step is to reconcile the
issuer-specific covenant definitions and, where disclosures permit, test how
revenue growth, operating margins and working capital influence those three
questions.
