# Project brief — European Defence: Valuation, Order-Book Growth & Credit Analysis

## Research question

How do Rheinmetall, Hensoldt and Renk compare on order-book growth,
profitability, leverage, debt-servicing capacity and valuation? Which company
appears most attractive from a risk-adjusted perspective, and what could
invalidate that conclusion?

## Why this peer group

The three companies are direct beneficiaries of higher European defence
spending, but have meaningfully different exposure: ammunition and land
systems, sensors and defence electronics, and mobility systems. This supports
a focused peer comparison while leaving genuine differences in margins,
backlog conversion, capital intensity and valuation.

## Scope

- Reporting period: the latest completed fiscal year available consistently for
  all three companies when data collection starts.
- Currency: retain each reported currency in the raw-data file; use reported
  multiples or an explicitly documented FX conversion for peer comparisons.
- Valuation: enterprise-value multiples, beginning with EV / EBITDA. Market
  capitalisation inputs must state their observation date.
- Credit: Net Debt / EBITDA and EBIT / Net Interest Expense. Definitions from
  each company must be captured, not assumed comparable without a note.
- Sector metrics: order intake, order backlog and backlog / revenue. These
  should be interpreted with delivery capacity and timing, not as revenue.

## Deliverables

1. A source register with a URL, page number, reporting period and extraction
   note for every material input.
2. A transparent comparable-company table.
3. A two-page investment memo containing thesis, valuation, credit view,
   order-book view, catalysts and risks.
4. Reproducible Python calculations and an optional dashboard.

## Guardrails

- Use public information only; never upload employer information, customer
  data, internal reports or credentials.
- Never present the work as investment advice.
- Flag non-comparable accounting definitions rather than forcing a comparison.
- Do not describe public defence spending or geopolitical events as a certain
  earnings outcome; distinguish facts, assumptions and scenario analysis.
